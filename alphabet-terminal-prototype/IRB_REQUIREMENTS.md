## 2. Research Design

### 2.1 Purpose and Objectives
To evaluate whether adaptive training using Expected Information Gain (EIG) algorithms improves learning and retention of structured knowledge compared to traditional linear training methods.

### 2.2 Background and Significance
Many cognitive tasks require learning structured relationships (e.g., software navigation, procedural sequences). Traditional training often linearizes this knowledge, limiting flexibility. This study tests an adaptive system that builds more flexible mental representations.

### 2.3 Study Duration
- **Per Participant**: 5-7 sessions over 1-2 weeks
- **Total Study**: 6 months from first to last participant

## 3. Participants

### 3.1 Inclusion Criteria
- Age 18-65 years
- Fluent in English
- Normal or corrected-to-normal vision
- Basic computer proficiency

### 3.2 Exclusion Criteria
- Diagnosed learning disabilities
- Uncorrected visual impairments
- Prior participation in similar alphabet learning studies

### 3.3 Vulnerable Populations
**None** - Study excludes minors, prisoners, and other vulnerable populations

### 3.4 Sample Size
- **Target**: N = 90 (30 per condition)
- **Minimum**: N = 60 (20 per condition)

### 3.5 Recruitment Methods
- University participant pool
- Campus flyers/digital boards
- Online research participation platforms
- No direct solicitation

## 4. Procedures

### 4.1 Screening (5 minutes)
1. Online eligibility questionnaire
2. Vision screening questions
3. Computer proficiency check

### 4.2 Informed Consent (10 minutes)
1. Present consent form electronically
2. Opportunity for questions via email/chat
3. Electronic signature required
4. Copy provided to participant

### 4.3 Experimental Sessions

#### Session 1: Baseline (45 minutes)
- Demographics questionnaire
- Working memory assessment
- Pre-training assessment on synthetic alphabet

#### Sessions 2-6: Training (30 minutes each)
- Randomization to condition
- Adaptive/Linear/Yoked training tasks
- ~100 trials per session
- Performance feedback after each trial

#### Session 7: Post-test (45 minutes)
- Immediate post-training assessment
- Transfer task (isomorphic domain)
- Strategy questionnaire

#### Session 8: Retention (30 minutes, 1 week later)
- Delayed retention test
- Debriefing

### 4.4 Data Collection Methods
- **Behavioral**: Response times, accuracy via custom software
- **Self-report**: Demographics, strategy use
- **No physiological/biometric data collected**

## 5. Risks and Benefits

### 5.1 Risks
**Minimal Risk** - No greater than everyday activities

#### Potential Risks:
- **Mild fatigue** from computer tasks
- **Mild frustration** from difficult items
- **Eye strain** from screen viewing
- **Boredom** from repetitive tasks

#### Risk Mitigation:
- Breaks offered every 10 minutes
- Adjustable difficulty to prevent excessive frustration
- Reminder to blink and look away from screen
- Can withdraw at any time without penalty

### 5.2 Benefits

#### Direct Benefits:
- Improved learning strategies
- Cognitive training effects
- Contribution to science

#### Societal Benefits:
- Advance understanding of learning
- Improve educational technology
- Inform adaptive training systems
